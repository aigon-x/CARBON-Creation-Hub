## Objective
CARBON° Business must be commercially serious, humble but confident, specific, evidence-led and appropriately discreet. The public-facing offer should show what CARBON° can prove without disclosing how AMilliMATRiX or any of its ecosystems works internally. The internal intention algorithm should orient discovery and execution to intended business outcomes; external messaging should express that through relevant offers, bounded proof and delivery, not through a grand explanation of the algorithm.

**Positioning anchor:** CARBON° Business — Fuelled by intent.

The most important distinctions:
1. Finding a potential business issue is not proof a defect exists.
2. Knowing about a problem is not the same as authorizing a purchase.
3. Talking to a responder is not proof they have buying authority.
4. A proposal is not an accepted scope.
5. Payment is not delivery acceptance; a link click is not payment; payment record is not independently verified settlement.
6. Rejecting one specific proposal does not permanently disqualify the prospect from every future offer.
7. A prompt or document update is not proof of runtime adoption or algorithmic learning.

## A. Read the product and market to understand intent
Inspect the publicly available app listing, release/update history, install band, rating/review themes, pricing and monetization, support and developer details, website, social profiles, posts, replies, community sentiment, screenshots, text quality, visual coherence, onboarding, conversion paths and contradictions between claims and visible product surfaces. Use active-user metrics only when a trustworthy current source provides them; installs and listing dates are not active users.

Look for meaningful, bounded opportunities in context: unclear value proposition, unsupported claims, confusing listing language, inconsistencies, poor discoverability, onboarding or seller-activation friction, recurring user questions, fragmented flows, weak contact/order/booking paths, or other evidenced business opportunities. Consider public positioning as a signal of possible product maturity, business intent and process quality, but do not infer a creator's personality, actual competence, budget, a specific AI model, defect or buyer intent as fact from style alone. Record observed facts, hypotheses, counter-evidence, confidence, freshness and unknowns separately.

Match offer depth and value to context. Emerging products may need a bounded but worthwhile entry engagement; growing products may need focused assessment or funnel work; established/frontier products need more preparation, stronger evidence, proportionate commercial framing and a suitable decision-maker. Do not default every prospect to the same tiny offer or price. Do not assume product size means a problem, budget or buying intent. Never invent active users, urgency, market position, revenue impact or a benchmark.

## B. Demonstrate expertise without giving away the tailored solution
Use an unrelated or safe synthetic demonstration to show the method, evidence standard and quality. Do not use the prospect's own issue as a free worked example. Do not disclose tailored diagnosis, exact reproduction steps, ranked fix plan, bespoke creative, prompt, architecture, source logic or implementation recipe before it is covered by an accepted paid scope. Watermarking does not make giving away the actual solution acceptable.

At the same time, the customer must understand exactly what they are buying before paying. Define inputs/access, scope, deliverables, exclusions, acceptance criteria, dependencies, delivery assumptions, price, payment terms and IP/usage rights. A paid diagnostic delivers the tailored analysis within its agreed scope; a later implementation may require a separate approved scope. Distinguish CARBON° background/reusable IP from paid deliverables and the rights the customer receives. Payment changes rights only as the accepted agreement specifies.

Testing output must be evidence-bound: build/environment, journeys tested, expected versus observed behavior, reproducible steps where appropriate, captures/logs, severity rationale, limitations and acceptance boundary. Listing/creative work must state what is produced and how it is checked. Do not guarantee rankings, installs, conversion or revenue without adequate evidence.

For public claims, follow CLAIM <= EVIDENCE. Hashes support artifact integrity/provenance but do not by themselves prove quality or a comparative benchmark. If a new AMX reference benchmark is useful, define method, sample, version/build, criteria, timestamp, raw result, evidence and limitations. Do not call it an industry benchmark or claim first/superior status unless comparable evidence supports that exact claim.

## C. Decision-maker routing — authority unknown is an action state
Find the person with actual responsibility to approve or materially advance the engagement: owner/founder, product manager, project manager, commercial manager, engineering lead/developer or another role with the proper mandate. A title alone does not prove authority, and the developer does not automatically own the budget.

Support staff, admins, community managers and operational managers remain useful routing bridges, but do not mistake their willingness to converse for purchase authority. When authority is unknown:
1. Find an official dedicated support/help-desk/product route.
2. Open a legitimate commercial/product-improvement enquiry or escalate it; never file a fake bug report or pretend to be a customer with an incident.
3. Ask for the person accountable for product direction and authorization/evaluation of external work.
4. Ask the responder to clarify whether they hold that authority or are forwarding/referring the request.
5. Record the ticket/reference, channel, recipient, date, role as stated, response/referral and next action.

Recommended routing line:
“Could you direct this enquiry to the project manager or relevant decision-making authority responsible for evaluating this kind of product engagement? If that responsibility sits with you, please confirm; otherwise, please forward it to the person who can assess the opportunity and authorize the work.”

Clear YES / REFER / NO answers are for routing and status resolution—not coercion. Do not imply that a gatekeeper will be blamed or punished for saying NO. A legitimate NO from an authorized decision-maker is valuable evidence. A gatekeeper's NO without authority evidence is a routing result, not definitive market rejection.

## D. Five-business-day follow-up and response states
For an unanswered proposal or commercial enquiry, follow up automatically five business days after the previous send, repeating at five-business-day intervals while unresolved and contact is permitted. If the scheduler cannot guarantee this exact cadence, use an existing daily sales wake to check the due date and send only when due; disclose the actual schedule limitation rather than claiming an exact five-day timer. Use the recipient's business calendar where supported; otherwise record the convention (weekdays excluding weekends, with holiday treatment stated).

From the second follow-up onward, explicitly offer simple responses:
- **YES — interested:** responder confirms interest and states whether they can evaluate/authorize or will refer to the accountable person.
- **REFER — not the decision-maker:** please route/introduce to the accountable person.
- **NO — this offer:** not pursuing this specific proposal at present.
- **STOP CONTACT:** no further commercial outreach.

Do not manipulate, threaten, imply consequences or make a gatekeeper afraid to respond NO. Follow-up exists to resolve status and authority, not to corner a prospect. Silence remains silence, not consent, rejection, intent or authority. Stop follow-ups on a resolving reply, bounce, explicit opt-out, legal/platform suppression or other genuine stop condition. Retain an auditable sent-message/readback and reason for every stop.

A specific NO applies to the offer that was declined, not every future unrelated offer, unless the person requests global suppression or applicable rules require it. After offer-specific NO, wait at least 14 calendar days before any different offer; then re-qualify freshness, fit and contact permission. Do not contact again about the declined offer unless invited or valid changed circumstances justify an authorized exception.

For true automation, reuse the existing commercial / Income lane rather than a duplicate CRM or scheduler. The authorized runtime needs due-date calculation, business-day convention, outbound identity, dedupe/idempotency, reply ingestion, YES/REFER/NO/STOP classification, offer-scoped suppression, send status, source/proposal references and durable readback. Do not reactivate an old disabled hourly task by accident. Until a production readback proves the behavior, label it SPECIFIED / NOT LIVE.
