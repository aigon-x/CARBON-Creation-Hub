## Finding
The public-safe draft `AMX_PUBLIC_EVIDENCE_DECK_8SLIDE_DRAFT.pdf` repeatedly foregrounds non-claims about buyer acceptance, invoices, payment, and revenue. The distinctions are legitimate; the repetition makes the commercial story defensive and distracts from demonstrated capability and buyer value.

Examples in the inspected eight-page draft:
- Page 1: distinguishes build, test, deployment, buyer acceptance, and payment before the evidence story begins.
- Pages 3–4: repeats buyer acceptance/payment caveats alongside bounded test/acceptance evidence.
- Page 7: says “Tested is not deployed; deployed is not accepted; accepted is not paid.”
- Page 8: defines the payment evidence boundary again.

The draft itself supports only that it is a public-safe draft and does not claim realized revenue. It does **not** establish that AMX is unpaid or not getting paid. Do not convert absence of payment evidence in this artifact into a claim about actual payment status.

## Root cause / trust risk
A valid truthfulness safeguard has drifted into repetitive negative framing. This risks making AMX look commercially weaker than the evidence warrants, burying the offer and proof beneath defensive disclaimers, and confusing “not established by this artifact” with “did not happen.”

## Corrective rule — commercial confidence without overclaiming
1. Lead with the buyer problem, offer, demonstrated capability, bounded results, and relevant business value.
2. State a commercial milestone only when attributable evidence supports that specific claim.
3. Include a qualification only where it materially changes interpretation of the claim, is required by the channel, or prevents a likely misunderstanding.
4. Do not repeat generic “not accepted/not paid/no revenue” caveats across posts, profiles, pitches, captions, decks, and handovers by default.
5. Never infer non-payment from missing evidence; never invent payment, revenue, contract, or buyer acceptance.
6. Keep detailed evidence-state definitions in the appropriate supporting/technical material. For public sales copy, use one concise, context-specific boundary only when necessary.
7. Treat internal blockers, missing connectors, worker mechanics, and governance limitations as internal unless transactionally relevant. Do not leak them into external narrative.
8. Review existing copy prospectively and when touched; do not silently overwrite or delete the current deck. The current draft must remain intact as requested. Any replacement is a separate, versioned candidate requiring Owner inspection/authorization before publication.

## Recommended replacement principle
**Show what the evidence proves. Qualify only what the reader could reasonably misunderstand. Do not advertise what the evidence does not prove unless that boundary is material to the specific claim.**
